A ramen house that makes its own noodles.

Sam Morikizono took over a small restaurant on Convoy Street in 2001. It was already called Tajima. Twenty-five years later the noodles are cut in our own room in Crown Point, and the broth is simmered there every morning before service.

The art of the noodle.

Ramen making is a science of size and texture. A Tajima noodle is firm, fresh and springy. That comes from several types of flour, and water held at the right pH level.

Different bowls want different noodles. A thin, straight noodle carries a clean tonkotsu. A thicker, wavier one holds up to miso and sesame. We match the noodle to the broth, which is the part most people never see and always taste.
